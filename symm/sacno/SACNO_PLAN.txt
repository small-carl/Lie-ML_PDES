## 0. Thesis & novelty in one paragraph

Symmetry-informed machine learning for PDEs has a documented central failure mode:
when symmetry is imposed as a SOFT LOSS PENALTY ||X(u_theta)||^2, the penalty (and
its gradient) VANISH exactly at points already consistent with the symmetry, so the
network gets "no training signal" precisely where the constraint should bite
(Generalized Lie Symmetries in Physics-Informed Neural Operators, arXiv:2502.00373).
SAC-NO removes that failure mode STRUCTURALLY rather than fighting it: build the
neural operator on the ORBIT-QUOTIENT — feed it the Lie-INVARIANT (similarity)
coordinates, not the ambient (x,t).  Then equivariance is a property of the
COORDINATE MAP, exact before training begins, independent of seed, data, or loss.
The headline, verified result: an invariant-coordinate network has equivariance
error ~1e-16 (machine epsilon) while an identical raw-coordinate network has ~1e0;
the "no-gradient-at-symmetric-points" pathology is simply absent because the group
has been divided out upstream of the network.  On top of this, a SINGLE
similarity-anchored operator learns an entire one-parameter symmetry ORBIT of
solutions and generalizes to held-out members at rel-L2 ~4e-3.  The novelty is the
seam SAC-NO occupies (§1): equivariance by INPUT COORDINATES + DATA GEOMETRY, as
opposed to by loss (Generalized-Lie-PINO), by compact-group architecture (G-FNO),
or by inference-time canonicalization (LieLAC).


## 1. Positioning — the empty seam (prior art locked from the ref/ corpus)

The design space for "Lie symmetry + neural operator" has three occupied corners and
one empty one.  SAC-NO is the empty one.  (All four papers are in ../../ref/.)

  [P1] Generalized Lie Symmetries in PINOs (arXiv:2502.00373).  Imposes symmetry as
       a LOSS PENALTY.  The paper itself states architecture-embedding of PDE point
       groups is hard and that GENERALIZED symmetries are "unsuitable for ...
       equivariant architectures" — so they choose loss.  Documents the no-signal
       pathology.  The words "similarity" and "reduced" appear 0 times.
       --> SAC-NO's contrast: we do NOT use a loss; we change coordinates.

  [P2] G-FNO (Helwig et al., arXiv:2306.05697).  Group-equivariant Fourier neural
       operator — but EQUIVARIANT ARCHITECTURES for COMPACT groups (rotations/
       reflections of the grid).  PDE similarity groups (scaling, Galilean,
       projective) are NON-COMPACT and act on the coordinates, not as grid
       permutations.  --> SAC-NO handles exactly the non-compact similarity groups
       G-FNO cannot embed.

  [P3] LieLAC — Lie Algebra Canonicalization (in ref/, "...under arbitrary Lie
       Groups").  An INFERENCE-TIME canonicalization WRAPPER around a pretrained
       black-box operator: map input to a canonical orbit representative, run the
       net, map back.  --> SAC-NO bakes the canonicalization into the TRAINING
       coordinate chart, so the operator is trained ON the quotient, not wrapped
       after the fact; no separate canonicalizer to fit or invert at test time.

  [P4] Geometric Neural Operators via Lie-Group-Constrained Latent Dynamics (ref/).
       Constrains LATENT dynamics by a Lie group.  --> different lever (latent
       dynamics) than SAC-NO (input coordinate chart + data on the quotient).

  The seam: NONE of [P1]-[P4] builds the operator's INPUT MANIFOLD as the
  similarity-reduced orbit quotient of a NON-COMPACT PDE symmetry, trained on
  symmetry-generated families.  That architecture+data seam is SAC-NO.
  (Re-confirm all four characterizations against the PDFs before final write-up;
  the "0 occurrences of 'similarity'" claim for [P1] should be re-grepped.)


## 2. Core idea & the mathematics

A Lie point symmetry group G of a PDE acts on the independent/dependent variables.
Its ORBITS foliate the (x,t,u)-space; the SIMILARITY VARIABLES are coordinates
CONSTANT along orbits (the orbit quotient).  Classical Lie reduction already uses
them to turn a PDE into a lower-dim ODE.  SAC-NO's move: use the SAME invariant
coordinates as the INPUT CHART of a neural operator.

  Equivariance-by-coordinates (the structural claim).  Let z = z(x,t) be a complete
  set of invariants of a one-parameter subgroup g_lambda (so z(g_lambda . p) = z(p)
  for all lambda).  Any network of the form N(z(x,t)) satisfies
        N(g_lambda . p) = N(p)    exactly, for all lambda, p, theta.
  Equivariance error is therefore zero by construction (up to floating-point
  evaluation of z), NOT a quantity to be trained down.  For subgroups that also act
  on u, compose with the (known, closed-form) cocycle rho_g on the output; the
  invariance argument is unchanged.  This is the structural removal of [P1]'s
  no-signal pathology: there is no symmetric point at which a loss gradient could
  vanish, because there is no symmetry loss.

  Orbit sheets as a 1-D operator input.  A one-parameter symmetry maps a single
  solution into a whole FAMILY (an orbit "sheet").  Anchored to the quotient, the
  family collapses to a smooth dependence on a single ORBIT LABEL; one operator
  N(z; label) can represent the whole sheet and interpolate/extrapolate in the
  label — the operator-learning payoff (§3.2).

  Relation to CCS-SYM+.  CCS-SYM+ produces, per problem, (i) the invariant
  coordinate z (its REDUCE stage) and (ii) a certified HAM series U_M(z;hbar*)
  (CLOSE+CERTIFY).  SAC-NO consumes BOTH: z is the operator's input chart; U_M is a
  candidate prior for the operator's CORRECT stage.  §3.3 reports HONESTLY which
  way of consuming U_M actually helps.


## 3. Verified probes (re-run on this CPU — the evidence base)

Three falsifiable probes.  Two confirm SAC-NO's claims; one is an honest negative
that BOUNDS a claim.  Scripts live in this folder.

### 3.1 Equivariance-by-coordinates is exact (HEADLINE — strong PASS)
  REPRODUCE: python symm/sacno/sacno_equivariance_probe.py
  Setup: heat/porous scaling group g_lambda:(x,t,u)->(lambda x, lambda^2 t, u),
  invariant z = x/sqrt(t).  Two IDENTICAL small MLPs (16-16 tanh) differing only in
  input: one reads z, one reads raw (x,t).  Equivariance gap = RMS over 4000 points
  x 7 group elements of |N(g.p)-N(p)| / RMS|N(p)|, untrained, double precision.
  RESULT (this CPU):
      invariant z along orbits: max drift 1.78e-15      (genuinely an invariant)
      equivariance gap, INVARIANT-coord net : 6.9e-17   <-- exact by construction
      equivariance gap, RAW (x,t)      net  : 5.8e-1     <-- O(1), structural
      ratio raw/invariant                   : 8.4e+15
  READING: the invariant-coordinate operator is equivariant to machine precision
  with ZERO training; the raw-coordinate operator is off by O(1).  This is
  seed-independent (it is a statement about the coordinate map, not the weights),
  so it is the most robust result in the program.  [The exact magnitude of the RAW
  gap depends on the sampling/range; the INVARIANT gap ~ machine-eps does not.]

### 3.2 One operator covers a whole symmetry sheet (PASS, faithful to ~3e-3)
  REPRODUCE: python symm/sacno/sacno_parametric_operator.py
  Sheet: Burgers travelling-wave kinks  U(z;k) = -k tanh(k z / 2 nu), nu=0.5,
  z = x - c t — a SCALING ORBIT of Burgers (X = 2t d_t + x d_x - u d_u acts as
  k -> k/lambda), every member closed-form so held-out error is exact.
  Operator: Linear(2,32)-Tanh-Linear(32,32)-Tanh-Linear(32,1) = 1185 params, reads
  the invariant coordinate z and the orbit label k.  Standard neural-operator
  protocol: supervised on 5 orbit labels, tested on a DISJOINT set.
  RESULT (this CPU):  train 1.2 s.
      rel-L2 on TRAINED labels   : 2.6e-3 .. 6.5e-3
      rel-L2 on HELD-OUT (interp): mean 3.7e-3, worst 6.0e-3   <-- generalizes
      rel-L2 on HELD-OUT (extrap): 2.3e-2 (k=1.7), 5.6e-2 (k=2.0)  <-- degrades, honestly
  READING: a single similarity-anchored operator learns the sheet from a handful of
  members and INTERPOLATES held-out members at ~4e-3; extrapolation beyond the
  training band degrades gracefully (reported, not hidden).  The symmetry collapses
  the family to a 1-D label the operator learns cheaply — whereas a plain PINN must
  be re-solved from scratch for each new k.

### 3.3 Curriculum: how to use the certified HAM prior (HONEST / BOUNDED)
  REPRODUCE: python symm/sacno/sacno_curriculum_warmstart.py
  Setting: Fisher-KPP reduced ODE at c=5/sqrt6 in the bounded Ablowitz-Zeppetella
  variable s=e^{z/sqrt6} in (0,1]; N_s[V]=s^2 V''+6sV'+6V(1-V)=0, exact V=1/(1+s)^2.
  Certified HAM series (built exactly in sympy, hbar* by residual-min in the
  certified band) truncated to M=4 -> an IMPERFECT prior (rel-L2 8.3e-2).  Two ways
  to use it, vs a cold random-init baseline, fair multi-seed (12) tight-budget LBFGS:
    (a) WEIGHT WARM-START (init the net to the prior, then short residual-train).
        RESULT: does NOT beat cold init on this well-conditioned ODE
        (budget 8: warm median 3.6e-2 vs COLD median 1.8e-2).  The cold linear
        secant through the exact BCs is already close; a few LBFGS steps converge
        it; an imperfect prior buys nothing.  The 10x warm-start advantage seen in
        an early scratch probe does NOT survive a fair test here.  -> BOUNDED CLAIM:
        warm-start value is regime-dependent (expected to help only when cold init
        genuinely fails: stiff / high-frequency / higher-dimensional reduced
        problems), left as future work, NOT claimed.
    (b) FROZEN ADDITIVE ANCHOR  U = U_prior + b(s)*net.
        RESULT (robust): PLATEAUS at 1.7e-1 (>= the prior's own 8.3e-2 error)
        because the boundary mask b vanishes at the ends, so the correction cannot
        fix the prior where it is wrong.  -> clean DESIGN RULE: never inject the
        certified series as a frozen additive anchor.
  SIDE RESULT (clean, used above): in the LOGISTIC coordinate xi = s/(1+s) =
  e^{z/sqrt6}/(1+e^{z/sqrt6}) in [0,1] (covering the WHOLE front, unlike s<1), the
  AZ wave LINEARIZES to U = (1-xi)^2, and the reduced ODE becomes
        [xi(1-xi)]^2 U'' + xi(1-xi)(6-2xi) U' + 6U(1-U) = 0,
  verified exactly (residual 0).  Useful as a bounded full-front chart for future
  warm-start tests; xi=0 is a regular singular point (Euler exponents -2,-3), which
  is why a naive L=d^2/dxi^2 HAM there diverges — documented, not a bug.


## 4. Phased plan (each phase: objective -> falsifiable gate)

  Phase S0  Formalize equivariance-by-coordinates.  Objective: write the lemma
    (invariant chart => exact equivariance) for the standard non-compact PDE groups
    (translation, Galilean, scaling, projective) incl. the output cocycle case.
    GATE: for each group, a script reproduces equivariance gap < 1e-12 (invariant)
    and > 1e-2 (raw), as in §3.1, on that group's invariants.

  Phase S1  Multi-parameter orbit sheets.  Objective: extend §3.2 from a 1-parameter
    sheet to a 2-parameter sub-sheet (e.g. Burgers Galilean x scaling) and to a
    NON-closed-form family (Fisher fronts at varying c via a solve_ivp reference).
    GATE: held-out rel-L2 < 1e-2 (interp) on the 2-param sheet; report the
    extrapolation cliff explicitly (no silent truncation).

  Phase S2  Strict-leak ablation.  Objective: remove the oracle-derived BC leak —
    train the operator with GENERIC far-field constants, not exact-wave BCs (the
    same strict variant flagged in ../ml_pde_solver_demo.py).  GATE: invariant-coord
    operator still generalizes across the sheet; quantify the accuracy cost of
    dropping the leak (honest delta, both arms).

  Phase S3  Warm-start regime map (turn the §3.3 negative into a boundary).
    Objective: find where, if anywhere, the certified-prior warm-start DOES beat
    cold — sweep stiffness / front sharpness / reduced-dimension.  GATE: either
    exhibit a regime with a reproducible warm-start advantage (>=3x, multi-seed) OR
    report a clean negative across the swept regimes.  Either outcome is publishable.

  Phase S4  Head-to-head vs the loss-penalty approach [P1].  Objective: on one
    shared PDE family, compare SAC-NO (invariant chart, no symmetry loss) against a
    matched operator trained with the ||X(u_theta)||^2 penalty.  GATE: SAC-NO
    matches or beats accuracy AND has structurally lower equivariance error, with
    the no-signal pathology demonstrated for the penalty arm and absent for SAC-NO.

  Phase S5  Write-up + McGreivy-Hakim-compliant evaluation.  GATE: every headline
    number reproduced from a script; strong (not weak) baselines; reporting-bias
    checklist (per ../../ref/ "Weak baselines ... overoptimism ...") passed.


## 5. Risks & honest bounds

  R1  "Invariant coords need a complete set of invariants."  For some groups the
      invariants are messy or the action is only local.  Mitigation: restrict the
      thesis to the classical similarity groups where invariants are explicit
      (the same scope as CCS-SYM+).  Bound, stated up front.
  R2  "Equivariance is exact but accuracy is a separate question."  §3.1 proves
      equivariance, NOT accuracy; accuracy is §3.2/§3.4's job.  Do not conflate.
  R3  "Warm-start may never pay off."  §3.3 already shows it does not on the easy
      benchmark.  Phase S3 is explicitly allowed to return a negative.  The
      additive-anchor rule (3.3b) is the robust, keepable result.
  R4  Extrapolation off the training sheet degrades (§3.2).  Reported as a cliff;
      do not market interpolation numbers as extrapolation.
  R5  Prior-art characterizations (§1) are from corpus reading; re-grep/re-read the
      four PDFs before any external claim of novelty.
